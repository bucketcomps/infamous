SPU float maths fixed to match the real console (review ACCEPT)

The SPU is the Cell co-processor that decodes the game's Bink movies. Our SPU interpreter was doing standard PC float maths, and the real chip doesn't: it has no infinity, treats the top exponent as an extra-large number, always rounds toward zero and clamps instead of overflowing.

  • The SPU is the Cell co-processor that decodes the game's Bink movies. Our SPU interpreter was doing standard PC float maths, and the real chip doesn't: it has no infinity, treats the top exponent as an extra-large number, always rounds toward zero and clamps instead of overflowing.
  • Measured against a volunteer console's captures (wrong answers before, then after the fix): add 656→0, multiply 1,103→0, subtract 730→0 (of 2,048 each), fused multiply-add family ~1,700–1,800→0, interpolate 21,378→0 (of 26,624).
  • Both of our SPU engines, the Python one and the fast C one, now agree bit-for-bit across 92,160 hardware cases plus 280,000 random ones.
  • Ten other questions the probe had already answered (number conversions, mffs, dcbz size, vector NaNs) are folded in, but only for inputs the hardware actually measured. Everything else still refuses.
  • An independent reviewer re-derived every count from the raw console data. It rejected the first round because the C engine was still wrong, then accepted the fix.
  • Next: boot with the corrected SPU to see whether the intro movie gets past its freeze. Untested so far; this is one console's samples, not all-input proof.
